# Athenaeum specification — changelog

One version, four parts (Part I §8). This file is the amendment index: what each version changed and why, compressed. The full story of any change — the measurements, the migration sweeps, the rejected alternatives — lives in git history (`git log -- spec/`) and under tags; the ruling trail lives in the tracker. The spec text itself carries current law only.

## The unified line

- **v37** (2026-08-23, owner ruling) — **The exclusion and the clean death.** Two rulings from one live strand: the 2026-08-23 Gmail takeout failed re-encounter on all 28 closed periods because Gmail auto-purges Spam/Trash after 30 days — measured drift between the two monoliths: 1,068 only-A members = 924 Trash + 35 Spam + 2 Drafts + 108 operator-hard-deleted inbox messages. (1) **`exclude_members`** (§12.3.13): a producer overlay MAY declare a member-exclusion predicate list beside `strip_headers` (gmail: `X-Gmail-Labels` contains Spam or Trash; Drafts stay) — a **policy filter, not canonicalization**: excluded members are captured nowhere, by design; the predicate reads the header before the strip removes it (read-then-strip, one pass); `mbox-split`, `mbox-window`, and export-diff apply the same resolved exclusion so strata, window, and measurement agree; per-bucket excluded counts + run total disclosed. This is what makes gmail's closed-period stability claim honest under `measured` onboarding — the purge-prone members were the instability. (2) **Container retirement: extend or die** (§12.8, `corpus retire`): every promoted member of a retiring container either re-points to a live container carrying its byte-identical bytes (origin fold — extend) or is removed with the container (die) — decided by bytes, one sweep, manifest-first with ledger impact disclosed up front (repoint citations before, or let verify fail loudly after). The third state is prohibited by name: no transferred-origin husks, no acknowledged tombstones, no routes to bytes that no longer exist — dead lineage is confusion bait and it festers. (This supersedes the v32 narrative's origin-transfer instinct as a general rule: that transfer was for a collapse migration where the same identity continued with its bytes in store; a stranded member whose bytes exist in no live container dies instead.) Consequence executed instance-side under this law: the one-time mail-lineage re-baseline from the 2026-08-23 export under the exclusion — re-cut strata become the settled baseline; the July strata, the monolith, and its 153 stranded unstripped rfc822 promotions retire per the sweep, evidence repointed under operator confirmation.

- **v36** (2026-08-22, owner ruling) — **Settled on first cut.** Temporal stratification (§12.3.14) gains its second onboarding mode, born from the Proton Mail export onboarding: the measurement gate's two-export diff proves closed-period *reproducibility*, but some producers neither offer nor need it — mail history that is append-only in practice, where the owner rules that a re-export reconciles nothing. `partition.onboarding: settled-first-cut` (beside the default `measured`): closed periods are cut **once, from the first export that covers them, and never re-cut or reconciled** — no byte-stability claim is made, so no measurement is owed; the **ruling** (owner, date, rationale) banks in the overlay's comment block where the measurement numbers would go. Operational discipline is part of the mode: settled strata are never re-ingested; each future export contributes only newly-closed periods and the rolling residue; even a member's later deletion at the provider reconciles nothing. The mode claims strictly less than `measured` (first-cut authority, not reproducibility) — member-dedup-only remains the floor for producers with neither a measurement nor a ruling. Riders in the same sweep, both Proton-shaped and both general: `corpus period-split`'s member↔sidecar **pairing convention becomes producer-declared**, resolved through the same `--origin` ladder as the partition schedule (osxphotos `<member>.json`; proton-mail-export `<stem>.metadata.json` ↔ `<stem>.eml`), with export-level metadata files that pair with no member (`labels.json`) excluded from bucketing and disclosed — never bucketed as undated primaries; and the sidecar **date axis accepts epoch-seconds integers** (UTC by definition, so the v34 boundary rule is satisfied trivially — no naive/aware question even arises). Migration: none — no existing overlay declares `onboarding:` (absence means `measured`, byte-identical semantics), and both riders are additive.

- **v35** (2026-08-22, owner ruling) — **The ordinal return.** The HTML address space (Part II §6.1.1) returns to a flat auto-increment spelling: `el=<N>` is the element's 1-based **document-order ordinal** (depth-first pre-order from the body), replacing the dotted child-index path — `el=1.2.2.1.3.1.4.1.8.3` becomes `el=87` — which had itself replaced the pre-path filtered-whitelist index (the 3.6 remap). Each generation's lesson is kept: **totality** survives untouched (every element addressable, no predicate to rot — the 3.6 amendment's substance), and pre-order adds that numeric order *is* document order and a subtree occupies a contiguous ordinal interval. The trade, made with eyes open: containment and siblinghood stop being decidable from two addresses alone and become questions for the attested parse — which measurement showed every live algebra consumer (span checks, envelope derivation, lint, verify) walked anyway. The motive is honest and stated: dotted addresses were unreadable and uncitable in practice, and an address space whose citations people read is an interface; ordinals also give interval anchors a natural spelling. Two owner riders are normative, both learned from the flat 3.5-era range's failure mode (ranges bridging elements at different depths): (1) **sibling-only ranges** — `el=[A-B]` is valid iff its endpoints share a parent element, claiming the sibling run subtrees-included; anything else is an invalid address, refused at resolution and lint error `address-el-range-invalid` when stored; (2) **address up, never across** — the mechanical envelope derivation prefers single subtree → contiguous sibling range → lowest common container element, and never emits a cross-structure bridge (or a list, which stays authorable for genuinely disjoint landmarks). Grammar dispatch stays record-owned, never value-sniffed: `addressing.scheme: ordinal` (§7.1) marks the new space; a stamp without the key is the frozen dotted era; an unstamped record the frozen whitelist era — three generations, all resolvable read-only. The amendment also lands the **authoring surface** (owner-ruled in the same sitting, in three strokes): ordinals are counted by machines, never by eyes — the **annotated view** stamps every element's ordinal into its own start tag as `data-el="<N>"`, **span-surgically** into the original bytes (the header-strip / `strip_fields` discipline: no re-parse, no re-serialization, full faithfulness by construction, provable by re-derivation) — and it is the **default delivery**: bare `corpus://<hash>` on a markup artifact resolves to the annotated view, because an agent left to choose would skip it and guess, and guessed addresses are the failure class the whole contract exists to end. `?raw` yields the stored bytes exactly (rarely needed), and the identity equation for markup keys on that route — the store is untouched, attestation still hashes original bytes. Enforcement: ordinal grammar in the functional-URI parser, tree-backed algebra in the HTML transform, drafters stamp the scheme, envelope derivation gains parse access, lint/verify/fidelity dispatch per record. Migration: fleet-wide, once, mechanical, in the §12.28-remap mold — pair each stored dotted path (or legacy index) with the ordinal of the *identical element*, prove every pair by re-resolution, rewrite all record surfaces (segments, rosters, placements, annotation contexts, chained forms) plus ledger `el=` anchors and URIs, restamp with the scheme; the engine lands with this amendment, instance execution separately operator-gated. Rider it moots: tracker #210 (`corpus body --anchor` rejecting dotted `el=`) dissolves with the dotted spelling.

- **v34** (2026-08-22, owner ruling) — **The UTC boundary pin.** Temporal stratification (§12.3.14, the monthly-container standard for re-delivered temporal exports — iMessage, Photos, mail, message platforms) never said which timezone cuts a period; the tooling bucketed by each date value's face reading (an email an hour into July by its `+0600` sender clock belongs to June's container, not July's). The ruling: **period boundaries are UTC calendar boundaries for every producer and every grain** — deliberately no per-source knob, because per-source boundaries silently duplicate or drop boundary-straddling members between strata that should agree. Offset-bearing date-axis values (RFC 5322 `Date:`, offset-bearing ISO timestamps) convert to UTC before bucketing; **naive** values (EXIF-style local times with no offset in the bytes) bucket at face value — an offset the bytes do not carry is never invented — and which of the two an axis yields joins the onboarding measurement's record. Close-out is restated as a consequence of membership, not a clock: a period closes at the first export after its UTC end, whenever that export runs, because only content dated within the period ever lands in its container. Enforcement: `corpus mbox-split` and `corpus period-split` share the one bucketing rule (aware → UTC, naive → face value). Migration: settled strata are grandfathered exactly as the `eras` schedule already provides (never re-cut by default); a future re-baseline that re-cuts history under the UTC rule may re-mint a boundary-straddling period once — narrow blast radius, ordinary mechanical supersession on member identity.

- **v33** (2026-08-22, owner ruling) — **The mbox precedent finishes what it started.** Born from the correspondence-family conformance probe of v32 (mbox ⊃ rfc822 ⊃ MIME parts, run against a live instance): `msg=`/`part=` member leaves already satisfied the payload-identity principle by construction — leaf id == blake3 of the un-stuffed / CTE-decoded member bytes, the identity equation verified live — but v32's two route rules were written narrower than the principle they rest on. Two generalizations, no new doctrine: (1) **Route unification generalizes to every member axis** (Part II §6.2) — a promoted member leaf resolves through its container whenever its containment-lineage origin is a member address of a blake3-pinned parent, `stream_id=`, `msg=`, `part=`, `path=`, `card=`, `entry=`, `item=` alike: one route, one derivation, one cache entry, base form disclosed on resolve. Measured pre-amendment: the leaf route and the container route minted duplicate cache entries for byte-identical content, with no `≡` disclosure outside media. (2) **Member re-chaining generalizes beyond `path=`** (§6.2) — a member materialized through any member axis re-detects its mime and, when a further transform follows, re-enters the working-kind table: `?msg=23&part=3` reaches an attachment through the mbox, the composition the precedent always implied. Measured pre-amendment: "transform 'part' not applicable to working kind 'bytes'". Rider from the same probe, landed ahead as a conformance fix under existing law (no amendment needed): a nested `message/rfc822` part's identity is the **verbatim embedded bytes** — §12.11 already defined part bytes as the CTE-decoded payload, and RFC 2046 pins a message part's CTE to identity — replacing the re-serialization engine (`as_bytes`) that had crept into the identity path. Migration: **none** — record shapes and member-leaf ids already conform; the pre-amendment duplicate cache entries are cache, disposable by definition. Store-wrapping remains the named follow-on direction.

- **v32** (2026-08-21, owner ruling) — **The payload-identity principle.** Born from a live strand: the v31 worked example's legacy migration held on roster-vs-streamed hash mismatches — seven promoted audio leaves whose single-track-container bytes, muxed under `-c copy -fflags +bitexact` (the 3.12 pinned-invocation regime), could no longer be reproduced after an ffmpeg upgrade; byte length identical, hash moved, records stranded. The ruling: **identity keys on the fully-unwrapped payload; wrappers are residence forms** (Part II §2). A member's identity bytes are the content with every wrapper removed — a media track's raw elementary stream in decode order (the container's own sample tables, concatenated), a compressed member's decompressed bytes — because *unwrapping* is a pure engine-free function of container bytes while *wrapping* is engine work that drifts across tool versions even pinned. Consequences, each normative: (1) the **identity equation** — `corpus://<leaf> ≡ corpus://<container>?<address>`, checkable by re-derivation, making §2's "every route yields identical bytes" a verifiable invariant; (2) **route unification** (§6.2) — a leaf resolves *through* its container, `corpus://<leaf>?<ops>` and `corpus://<container>?stream_id=<n>&<ops>` are one resolution, one cache entry, with the base form disclosed on leaf resolution; every playable/framed rendering is a derived version-labeled surface where engine drift is harmless; terminal `stream_id=` yields the payload; (3) the **`framing:` stamp retires** (§7.1) — no engine in the identity path means no producer to attest; the leaf MAY attest `samples:` directly; (4) the **single-track media container prohibition lifts** (§1.2) — payload identity dissolves the second-identity objection, so it is an ordinary one-row manifest, its wrapper retained for its tables; (5) **compressed single-member envelopes collapse at ingest** — the record minted is the payload's, the envelope hash attested as delivery provenance (§12.3.13 precedent), so the same content wrapped or bare mints the same record; (6) the §11 deferred item narrows from "deterministic stream extraction" (solved by construction) to Matroska/EBML reader coverage; §12.8's sample-sequence comparison becomes the migration's own exactness proof (old muxed leaf → payload leaf: byte-different, sample-identical). Migration: deliberate, fleet-wide, once — promoted-leaf members re-attest to payload hashes, leaves re-mint, rosters re-point (`ath ledger supersede`, whose roster rows rewrite unconditionally as of the v31 follow-up sweep); after it, re-derivation is id-stable forever. Migrating an *existing* envelope record (as opposed to ingest-time collapse, which stamps the capture origin on the payload directly) must also **transfer the envelope's producer origin onto the payload record** before the envelope is removed — a collapsed payload whose only origin block is containment lineage into a removed envelope is dangling lineage (`corpus health` flags it; coverage refuses to materialize the nonexistent target), learned live on the first migrated instance. **Named direction, not yet law**: store-wrapping — the artifact store holding bytes compressed or encrypted at rest under the same payload identity (the invariant becoming "decodes to the id'd bytes") — deferred to its own amendment on this foundation.

- **v31** (2026-08-21, owner ruling) — **The manifestation tier and the sweep declaration.** Two coupled growths, both born from one design ruling: the ontology stays split by nature — byte-shape law in the corpus, world-meaning in the ledger — and the discordance between them closes by *channel*, not by inversion. (1) **The representation layer** (Part III §4.2, §9, §4.4): the artifact roster is named as the **manifestation tier** — the concept is the work, each rostered record one representation of it; the relation is universal in principle, materialized on demand, and the record-shadow prohibition stands untouched. Roster entries gain four optional fields: `modality` (registered carrier vocabulary), `expression` (a version-grouping slug — a cut, an edition — promoted to a concept only when claims target the version itself), and `derived_from` + `derivation` (item-grain mechanical sameness — encode-of, remux-of — stated at the roster, never as a concept). Coverage (§9) gains **representation demand**: each uncovered record's backlog entry carries the prescribed work item (identify the work, stub it if new, roster the manifestation) — drainable frontier with a total horizon (eventually every in-scope record rostered or cited) on a demand-driven schedule; the reverse read (rostered-but-never-cited manifestations, per concept) lands beside it. Type schemas (§4.4) gain **`normalization_intent:`** — prose the citation discipline's enqueue carries down through the queue's `--hint` seam composed with the graph's own knowledge (participant identities as codebook lexicons), under proposes/disposes: the ledger proposes, the normalizer disposes against the bytes. Codebook positions become **durable** (Part II §7.8): a split appends and re-points, the conflated entry stays occupied and unreusable (the §4.1 lineage discipline at record scale), and re-pointing passes emit their migration worklist loudly. (2) **The sweep declaration** (Part II §4.3.3.6): the annotations zone's second namespace — `issue`'s positive twin. A `sweep` context block declares one extraction class (`text/ocr`, `text/transcript`, `structural`) carried to **exhaustion** over a band of the record's address space: inside the band, absence of a segment means absence of content; outside every sweep, extraction stays **sparse by default** and the bytes remain the band's representation. Fail-closed and earned (only the pass that processed the whole band may write one; `detector` + provenance per the issue mold), never an assertion of rendering (markers keep their meaning; formed spans need no sweep), and meaningful when empty (*no on-screen text in this range*). Same-kind sweeps never overlap; a widened re-sweep replaces its band. Sweep-backed **negative evidence** (citing an absence) is the named extension point in Part III §6. Spec-hygiene rider: worked examples carry generic placeholder identities only — the real addresses and operator paths the text carried are replaced. Migration: zero mandatory — no record carries a sweep (sparse is today's reading, now stated), and every existing roster entry remains complete as-is.

## The closed ATH-CORPUS line (≤3.14)

The corpus contract's own version line, closed at the v15 unification. Highlights; the numbered migration narratives (the former §12.16–12.37) are in git history (`spec/corpus-history.md` at its deletion commit, and the `pre-reforge` tag for older material).
