# Athenaeum specification — changelog

One version, four parts (Part I §8). This file is the amendment index: what each version changed and why, compressed. The full story of any change — the measurements, the migration sweeps, the rejected alternatives — lives in git history (`git log -- spec/`) and under tags; the ruling trail lives in the tracker. The spec text itself carries current law only.

## The unified line

- **v47.1** (2026-10-28, owner ruling; born from the carbonai Isometric Standard 2.2 certification and the Rulesets rule-engine consumer review) — **The text that holds it.** A **maintenance-line label** (owner ruling 2026-10-28): the unified line already holds v48–v52, so this amendment lands on the v47 maintenance line as v47.1 and is renumbered into the unified line at migration. Drafted on that line and **presupposes v49 §4.1** (claim-grain lineage rows, with its §1.4 row and §13.1 Graph clause) cherry-picked onto that line first. One ledger amendment: content a text prints is **one claim across every text state of its work**, and which text states an element is derived from its evidence, never written. (1) **Revision** (ledger §1.4, §4.2; Q1, Q9): the roster gains an optional **`revision`** — a distinct text state of a work (a patch, a draft, a certified text, a consolidation, a silent web or news edit, a dataset dump), witnessed by one or more records, ordered by **`against`**, carrying no intent and no status — named by its **tag**, taken from the document's printed identity (`2-1-2`, `d2026-10-27`, `c2026-06-21`, a printed update stamp kept as printed — `t2026-10-28T15:20`, its zone only if printed) else the observation time (`t2026-10-28T15:20Z` — only the capture clock carries `Z`); tags are unique within the work, and an omitted `revision` is the expression's sole text state. `expression` stays at version grain — what projects adopt and consumers pin — and becomes optional (unversioned works omit it); entries sharing both fields are interchangeable content. The v47 sentence "two entries sharing an `expression` are interchangeable content" was already false on carbonai: 2.1.0 and the patch-2.1.2 recapture roped under `v2-1`; a consultation draft and 1.0.0 under `v1-0` with 1 of 674 elements value-identical; `consolidated` riding as a second axis. Rejected: **"release"** (smuggles issuer intent and reads as "the current one" — a draft, a silent edit, or a dump is no release), **"snapshot"** (in this specification the capture event — several snapshots witness one revision, and an identical recapture is supersession), and redefining `expression` as the text state (scope would then hang off a grouping that is itself a claim). (2) **Expression ledger** (§4.2, §4.4; Q2): an array claim under the type's `expression_ledger: true` field, one element per rostered `expression` slug, satisfies the promotion requirement for claims about versions; an element's `change_summary` MUST cite printed release notes or changelog text — a summary derived from the ledger's own content is a view (it was an upward edge: the `isometric--standard` 2.2 summary was drawn from the claim notes it summarized). Rejected: promoting every expression to a concept (~2,600 new facts). (3) **Ranged claims** (§4.4, §5.1, new §5.6): a field MAY declare `ranged: true`; its claims carry `revisions` — one reconciliation per revision, `transcribed` (declared complete: every element holding at `against` is attested or closed there — the completeness rule that keeps lost evidence from silently retracting a rule) or `diffed` (differences only, the rest carried) — `closures` (evidence-bearing: absence cannot be quoted, so a cite of the revision's record, anchored at the replacement where one exists), and `corrections`. An element **holds** in a revision attested (evidence from it), carried (`diffed`, held at `against`, not closed), or pending-closure (`transcribed`, held at `against`, its only closure there an inert migration closure); `against` lives on the roster as the work's text order, `revisions[].against` is the claim's reconciliation base, required under `diffed` and wherever the roster orders a revision after one the claim already reconciles (no skipping the completeness rule); element order is append-only across revisions. A claim under a ranged field is **folded** once it carries `revisions`; an **unfolded** one (a pre-fold version-split claim) is fold-worklist frontier, never an error, read whole, with no projection and no `@`, and every ranged-claim rule applies to folded claims only. **Stratification**: holding reads the roster alone; version-history and lineage predicates MUST NOT be ranged; applicability ("in force for a project on *d*") is consumer logic over attributive facts — a text legislating its own applicability is ranged content an engine reads, never input to holding. Rejected: **per-version claim sets** (A, the v47 practice) — over the 20 facts holding ≥2 version-qualified claims of one array predicate, 8,693 later-version elements, 23% identical in both value and quotes, and cross-version identity recorded nowhere but in `isometric--standard`'s prose notes (×202); one A claim already spanned two texts with its closures written as prose; and **delta claims** (C) — a later version carrying only its changes as claims over its predecessor: a version's content becomes a replay of a chain, and no reference names one element across versions. (4) **Identity** (§4.4, §5.6, §6.1; Q4, Q5): an element continues while its `verbatim: true` keys print unchanged and its paraphrase keys stay a faithful reading (author judgment, backed by the revision's quote); a defined-term rename keeps the element and appends the quote; `locator: true` keys (the instance's `ref`) are never identity and ride evidence per revision as `locators`; otherwise close and append. An in-place edit is a correction only — it MUST hold at every revision the element is attested in, and MUST leave a trace (a `corrections` entry or a §7.3 correction). Rejected: **value-equality identity** — the two equality measures fail in opposite directions on real data (`isometric--model-quantification-enhanced-weathering` draft→1.0.0: 79 quote-identical, 1 value-identical; `isometric--cropland-management` 1.0→1.0.2: 575 value-identical, 6 quote-identical). (5) **Ladder and bar per revision** (§5.2–§5.4; Q3): `revisions[].status`, the claim-level status equal to the weakest; the bar per (element, revision); a carried element inherits its bar result from `against` under a `diffed` reconciliation that cites the revision's record (choice (a) — patches stay confirmed without per-element re-citation, the 141b1fbf practice); per-revision `asof` derived, the claim's the maximum; a projection carries its own revision's status, so a first-read 2.2 never downgrades what a 2.1 reader sees. Rejected: (b), attested evidence only (every patch provisional until re-anchored, about one evidence entry per element per patch). (6) **Visibility per projection** (§6.4; Q6): a private draft's evidence no longer privatizes the public revisions on its claim; the unprojected claim fails closed as before. Rejected: one claim per tenancy class. (7) **Element pins** (§7.3): `challenges` MAY name `element`, carrying `revisions: [<tags>]` — the set the pin was stamped over, refreshed by re-stamping — and hashing exactly the element's value, its closures, and the evidence bound to it from those revisions — so attestation at a new revision stops flagging every standing correction for re-review, while an edit to what the dispute examined still does. (8) **Projection and the URI grammar** (§11, §12, §12.1): `ledger://{id}:{short}[@{expression}[/{tag}] | @{tag}][#{N}]` is the normative **reference** form consumers write and pin; a consumer reads a ranged claim only through a projection; a cross-fact element reference MUST carry its own `@`; a frozen deliverable SHOULD pin the revision (`@v2-1/2-1-2`); an ambiguous or unreconciled selection, or a `#N` not holding there, is unresolvable, never a fallback — so rostering a new revision deliberately makes bare `@{expression}` references unresolvable on every ranged claim not yet reconciled to it, until it is, and a consumer pinned bare re-pins explicitly; invariants and scopes evaluate per projection; the RDF export keeps its own IRI form — a non-normative note maps reference to IRI (`{id}:{short}` → `{id}#{short}`, since a `:` after `//` parses as a port); `ref://{dataset}@{tag}` (§6.5, unchanged) and the ledger now share one `@tag` pinning grammar. (9) **The `ranged` claim-grain row** (§1.4, §4.1, §13.1; Q7): extends v49's claim-grain rows (`"file-id:short"` keys, resolving before the file row, one hop, occupied forever, a living `to`) with a third reason, `ranged` — a version-split claim folded into its ranged claim — and keys only such a row carries: the `elements` remap `{old: new}`, an optional `projection` the retired claim stood for, and an optional informational `qualifier` (the retired version qualifier as written, `"2.1"`, `"2.2.0"`, so the (fact, old qualifier) → projection mapping derives from rows without parsing shorts); a fold on a merge survivor also writes the row under each retired file id's spelling, so every old spelling resolves in one hop; a short is rewritten outside merge-collision only by a v49 move or this fold. (10) **Migration closures** (§5.6; Q8): closures written by the A→B merge carry `basis: migration`, stand only on `transcribed` revisions, and are inert until adjudicated — the element holds there as pending-closure and stays in the projection, which reports its mode. (11) **Supersession is not a revision** (§13.3): an unchanged recapture re-points; a changed one is a new revision, never superseded onto. Unversioned works (a news article, a live page) are a single tag chain, the terminal revision current by derivation, with the observation-interval caveat: a capture bounds when a text changed, never dates it. **Implementation readings** (dev-athenaeum + dev-corpus, 2026-10-28/29, owner-approved, landed with the tooling): a closure carrying a quote verifies as an evidence entry (§13.2); on an array-valued ranged claim a whole-value entry counts toward the bar but attests no element (§5.4); the unprojected claim's visibility reads closure and reconciliation cites as evidence, fail closed (§6.4); `transcribed` completeness holds within the claim's stated method, and a missed printed element is an omission repaired by attesting it at the revision — evidence appended, neither correction nor closure (§5.6; owner-approved 2026-10-29, raised by the isometric--standard re-fold, ~103 draft elements); a reconciliation's or closure's `source` references its sources entry as an evidence entry does — a revision cited only by its `diffed` row and closures leaves no unreferenced entry (§6.1; raised by a single-version sweep's unbumped silent edit). **Embedded script data** (owner-approved 2026-11-05; corpus §6.2, ledger §13.2): a new reading, `el=<N>&script` — a `<script>`/`<style>` element's own text, verbatim, escapes intact — so a fact a page carries only in its script data (a registry's per-version certification and publication dates, absent from the visible text) is citable and verifiable; element-scoped only, the quote carrying the identifying key of the object it reads, and `text` still drops script elements. Measured before landing: on 254 captured registry pages every version object sits whole inside one script element. Rejected: decoding the element's string literal so quotes read as plain JSON (it bakes a framework's serialization grammar into a corpus reading), and a whole-document payload reading (the record-wide matching §13.2 already calls misleading). Tooling: `corpus.transforms.html` registers `script` on `htmlel` (reading class); `resolver.can_yield_text` admits it from the registry. **Future work** (Q10, deferred): a registered `ref://` dataset snapshot as a revision of the dataset's work, `latest` its terminal revision — a follow-on amendment, not v47.1. - **v47** (2026-09-25, owner rulings; codex-steven R-0039, born from the R-0035 photo-library passes) — **The row the quote is in, and the fact not yet minted.** Two ledger amendments. (1) **Row-precise citation** (ledger §6.2, §13.1, §13.2): the anchor is the quote's scope, and a region anchor (one carrying `bbox=`) never scopes to its whole page — it scopes to the stored segment(s) at exactly that address, or, for a page region that is no stored address, to the page segments its region overlaps (overlapping none, it does not resolve); a bare axis anchor (`page=2`) still names the whole unit. A quote carrying `|` cites table **cells**, which verify within **one row** (a markdown pipe-table row or an HTML `<tr>`) where the scope holds a table — in-order matching across a page let a quote join one statement row's merchant to the next row's amount, and a `page=1&bbox=…` anchor naming the *Payments* table verified a purchase quoted from the *Purchases* table beside it. **An anchor is never prose**: every anchor param must name a known op or address axis (or the anchor is a `#fragment`) — a free-text anchor, which parsed as one unknown param and silently verified record-wide, is a check error (§6.2 already called it a defect; §13.1 now enforces it). Rejected: a region-clipped PDF text-layer reading (`page=N&bbox=<row>&text` — needs per-row geometry, and scanned statements have no text layer) and a `row=` axis inside a table segment (an address names a place in the bytes, not in the normalized rendering). (2) **Mint proposals** (ledger §4.2, §7.2, §7.3, §13.1): a hypothesis that a thing the ledger has no fact for exists carries **`proposes_new: {id, type, name, claims}`** — the concept to mint (`id` untaken while the hypothesis is open) and its draft claims (inline-cited like `proposes`); a `proposes` beside it may target the new fact or name it as `object`, and a draft claim's `object` must otherwise name a known fact (the dangling-object gap `proposes` had). `ath ledger promote` mints `facts/{type}/{id}.json` and lands every draft, validating everything before writing anything; `resolution` names the `proposes` claim, else the minted fact. Rejected: a warning in place of the error (promote still could not mint) and a stub minted ahead of the doubt (a stub asserts the existence the hypothesis doubts, §4.2). Tooling: `ledger.verify` (`scoped_text` exact/overlap region scope, `_quote_in_one_row`), `ledger.check` (`_anchor_unknown_param`, `proposes_new`, a shared draft-claim check), `ledger.promote` (atomic mint + land), `merge`/`verify` demand read `proposes_new` drafts; the `ledger-scribe` template's evidence and hypothesis guidance swept; `SPEC_VERSION` 47. **Migration** (instance; measured on ath-steven 2026-09-25 against a pre-v47 baseline of check 17 / verify 14 errors): `ath ledger check` +63 errors — the free-text anchors; re-anchor each at a stored address (`corpus toc`), or drop the anchor (a record-level cite is always safe) and move the words to `note`. `ath ledger verify` +47 errors, +60 warnings: 40 region anchors naming the wrong segment of their page — each has exactly one same-page stored segment holding its quote, so the re-anchor is mechanical — and 67 quotes whose cells span two or more rows (product spec tables, statement rows), each split into one evidence entry per row. No `proposes_new` exists yet; the prose mint proposals (about nine open hypotheses) may adopt it at leisure. Tooling beside it (R-0039, non-normative): a QuickTime `.MOV` attests its track manifest (QuickTime's version-1/2 sound description extends ISOBMFF's audio sample entry and nests `esds` in `wave`; the reader failed the whole container, and the drafter hid it as an empty roster — it now discloses an unreadable manifest as `partial-content/track-manifest-unreadable`; instance: `corpus reattest --mime video/quicktime`); `bbox` declared on the video address scheme (`frame=<t>&bbox=…`); ffmpeg children detached from the caller's stdin; a `path=` refusal points at the raw spelling when the value carries escapes §6.1 does not decode (`%20` is literal by law); the `members` cache keys on the record's roster and sidecar declaration; contact-sheet globs match case-insensitively.

- **v45** (2026-09-22, owner ruling on the R-0035 photo-library pilot review; codex-steven R-0037) — **The sidecar is its frame's.** Tightens v41. A container-member sidecar (§7.2 `sidecar:` — an osxphotos `<member>.json`) is **metadata of its frame, never a member**: (1) **off the roster** (§4.3.1.4) — attestation omits every entry the declaration pairs to a present primary, so it has no member route (§12.9), no promotion, and **no address of its own**: `?path=<sidecar>` is refused, naming the frame. Its bytes stay in the container (identity and `member_count` are physical), and an entry whose frame is absent from the container stays an ordinary member. (2) **Read through the frame** — a new reading-class op, `?path=<frame>&sidecar` (§6.2; bare `?sidecar` on a promoted frame via route unification), serves the sidecar's bytes verbatim, version-labeled `sidecar@1`; it is the form a ledger anchor quoting the sidecar takes. The `members` reading carries each frame's sidecar projection (the exact prefixed fields promotion lifts — one projection) and is relabeled `members@2`. (3) **Physical pairing** — the template is checked against the container's own entries, never the roster; promotion's refusal now runs before the roster lookup. (4) **Two sources, never reconciled** — a promoted frame's artifact block attests its own EXIF from the bytes and its origin block carries the sidecar lift; disagreement is ledger judgment. (5) **`corpus contact-sheet`** (§12.9.3, an instrument): a labelled grid over a container's image members — tiles are the frames' own `auto_orient&fit` renderings, selection is a path glob plus `FIELD<op>VALUE` predicates over the `members` descriptors (so a sidecar's lifted fields filter and sort), pages walk long sequences, the legend names every tile's member address and discloses what was passed over; cached as `contact-sheet@1`. Rejected: a tolerant alias keeping `?path=<sidecar>` resolvable (a husk — the owner's no-dead-lineage rule); a `sheet=` URI op (`sheet=` is already the spreadsheet address axis). Lint: `sidecar-member-rostered` (error) — a pre-v45 roster still listing paired sidecars. **Migration** (instance): `corpus reattest` every container whose origin declares a `sidecar:` — `corpus reattest --origin <overlay-id> --mime application/zip`, the new `--origin` selector (§12.4.6) reaching a uri-less producer-declared origin — which clears the lint; rewrite ledger anchors `path=<frame>.json` → `path=<frame>&sidecar` (evidence anchors and `based_on` URIs; quotes unchanged — the bytes are the same). Until remapped, such anchors verify as honestly unverifiable, with the refusal as the reason.
- **v44** (2026-09-21; born from the ath-steven Meta re-baseline, sjrahn/athenaeum#30) — **The index the export keeps.** `corpus period-split` gains a third date axis (§12.3.14): **index-file** — `--date-from index:<dotted-path>` reads each member's date from its entry in ONE index file the export keeps keyed by member path, for a producer that ships no per-member sidecar (Meta/Instagram: `stories.json` is `{ig_stories: [{uri, creation_timestamp, …}]}`, `posts_N.json` a list of posts each with `media: [{uri, creation_timestamp}]`; member mtimes there are the export-run time, so the mtime axis is dishonest). The structure is a **`date_index:`** declaration on the origin overlay (§7.2) — `file` (a glob over member paths), `entries` (dotted path to the entry array, arrays traversed), `key` (the entry field holding the member path, matched exactly) — resolved through the same ladder as `partition:`; the verb names only the date field, as the sidecar axis does. Value rules are the sidecar axis's verbatim: an epoch integer is UTC (v36), an offset-bearing ISO string converts (v34), a naive one is face-value unless a `render_timezone` applies (v38). Index members are never primaries — excluded from bucketing and disclosed (`date_index_files`) like `pairing.export_level`; entries naming no member (`date_index_unmatched`) and members the index dates two ways (`date_index_conflicts`, bucketed undated, never guessed) are counted on every emitted sidecar. Not added: a path-segment axis (the directory's own `YYYYMM`) — the directory is the producer's bucketing in an unstated zone, the index carries the instant; the smaller change was the less honest one. Migration: none — additive; no overlay declares `date_index:` yet. Tooling: `schemas.resolve_date_index`, `period_split` index loading + disclosure, `SPEC_VERSION` 44.
- **v43** (2026-09-02, owner ruling; born from the ath-steven mailbox scribe) — **The text behind the markup, and the reference inside the message.** Two derivation-op additions and one clarification; no migration. (1) **`text` on HTML** (§6.2): the markup's own text reduction — document order, block-aware line breaks, `<head>`/`<script>`/`<style>` dropped, whitespace normalized, nothing removed on judgment (no chrome strip, no quote trim, never a rendering) — whole-document (`?text`) and element-scoped (`el=<N>&text`, the span-precise form). Mechanical and unversioned like the PDF text layer's `page=<N>&text`. Its honest population is markup that is not presentation soup: a message-borne HTML body (`msg=<N>&part=<M>&text`), an element. The `segments` citation surface (§7.1) still governs a captured page — the whole-document reduction of a page is exactly the soup that surface refuses. Motivation, measured: 76 of 274 messages in one real Gmail month are HTML-only, and `block=1` (reply-only, quoted history trimmed) is the wrong surface for a forwarded booking. (2) **Intra-container references** (§6.2): an HTML carrier whose source is a `cid:` URI (RFC 2392) names a sibling MIME part by Content-ID — a member reference inside the message, materialized through the enclosing message exactly as a member address is, never inlined into stored bytes; the mail-axis counterpart of the `data:` URI a web capture bundles. Rejected alternative: inlining at `mbox-split`/`mbox-window` — a re-serializing engine in the identity path (the v32 lesson), every closed stratum re-minting, for 8 of 267 HTML bodies in the measured month (none of them HTML-only; zero `data:` URIs anywhere). (3) A container member's `el=` (no record, no stamp) speaks the current ordinal grammar (§6.1.1) — the frozen grammars are for stored addresses, and a member has none. Tooling: `transforms.html` gains `text` on `html`/`htmlel` and `cid:` materialization (image and non-image carriers) fed by the `part=` handler's enclosing-message context; `_rechain_member` stamps the ordinal scheme on a member's HTML. Landing alongside as a conformance fix, not an amendment: the terminal-textual decode of a MIME part (`part=<N>` on `text/*` prints text per §6.2's existing rule; declared Content-Type and charset honoured; new pin `eml-part@1`) — the defect that left `msg=<N>` citations unverifiable. (4) **Op classes** (§6.2; `ledger.md` §6.2, §13.2) — the ruling this amendment closes on: not every derivation op is a citable surface. Every op — transformation, record-level op, bare address axis — belongs to exactly one class, declared once per op name in the tooling's registry (an unclassified op cannot register): **address** (names a place — selectors, plus the frame a region is measured in and the disclosed `cover=` subtraction), **reading** (the mechanical text of an addressed place — `text`, `body`, `members`, `outline`), **view** (renderings and their configuration, the `annotated`/`raw` delivery forms), **instrument** (measurements and proposals about the artifact — `probe`, `words`, `geometry`, `scene`, `scenes=`, `bands`, a contact sheet), **engine** (`transcribe`: citable only once consumed into the record, §6.4). Two normative consequences: a stored address is address-class only (lint `address-op-class`, error — measured zero violations on the reference instance before landing); an evidence anchor is address-class ops optionally ending in one reading, and a view/instrument/engine op in an anchor is a citation defect at the claim's severity, checked before any resolver call — never the honest-unverifiable path. `corpus inspect`'s per-mime op list prints each op's class beside its pin: what is available, and its place.
